# LOGIC NEXUS — MASTER SCRIPT (structure pass)
## **Could L Catch Spider-Man?**

**Continuity:** MCU Peter Parker after *No Way Home*
**Narration begins at 0:00. No intro. No logo. No music-only lead-in.**

*Structure pass on top of the voice pass. Same story and same beats. What changed:*
- *The first two sections are merged and cut by about 40%.*
- *Peter now appears by about minute 3, through short cutaways.*
- *Repeated explanations are trimmed throughout.*
- *The "darker version" moves ahead of the verdict.*
- *The long recap is cut to about 60 seconds.*

---

### 0:00 — HOOK

The whole world forgot Peter Parker.

L doesn't need them to remember.

Because every night Spider-Man puts on the mask, he makes the same mistake.

He acts like Peter Parker.

He picks which crimes to stop. He picks which way to go home. He uses gear somebody has to build and fix. And he disappears for hours at a time, because under that mask there's still a guy with a life.

L has cracked cases with a lot less.

But Peter's got something L has never had to deal with.

Spider-Sense.

It tells him something's wrong before he even knows what.

So L can't tail him. He can't sneak up on him.

And the second Peter figures out someone's studying Spider-Man, every pattern L finds turns into a pattern Peter can fake.

A detective rebuilding a man the whole world forgot…

against a superhero who realizes someone's rebuilding him.

And here's the strange part.

L can figure out exactly who Spider-Man is…

and still have no way to prove it.

So let's hand L the case.

No Avengers files. No magic explanation for Doctor Strange's spell. Nobody who remembers that Peter Parker was Spider-Man.

Just the evidence New York already has.

Could L catch Spider-Man?

Let's find out.

---

# 1 — THE RHYTHM AND THE BILLS

L's first move is to skip the obvious one.

He doesn't search for Spider-Man's identity. Not yet.

Search eight million people for a guy in a mask and you get eight million suspects.

So L rebuilds Spider-Man's life instead.

And the first thing he wants is time.

He pulls every confirmed sighting from the last few months. Police reports. 911 calls. News choppers. Traffic cams. Anything with a time and a place he can check.

Then he throws most of it out.

Fake sightings. Gone.

Random posts with no proof. Gone.

Guys in Halloween costumes. Gone.

L doesn't want more data. He wants data he can trust.

And what's left shows something weird.

Spider-Man isn't always around.

There are gaps. Big ones.

This guy crosses half of New York without hitting traffic. He can go for hours.

So why does he keep vanishing?

L breaks the week into hours.

When something huge happens, Spider-Man shows up whenever.

But regular patrols keep a schedule.

Big disasters tell L where Spider-Man **can** go.

Regular patrols tell him where Spider-Man **chooses** to go.

So the gaps aren't about power.

They're about life.

He sleeps. He eats. Maybe he's got a job. Maybe he's in school.

L writes his first note:

**The guy under the mask controls the schedule.**

And right away, he tries to prove himself wrong. Weather? Police shifts? Traffic?

A few line up.

Most don't.

So now L has something. No name. No address.

A rhythm.

And you can hide your face a lot easier than the rhythm of your life.

Then L puts old Spider-Man footage next to the new stuff.

Old Spider-Man: huge public fights, other powered heroes, big-money tech.

New Spider-Man: quieter. Local. Alone.

Something cut him off from whoever used to back him.

For L, that's great news.

Because a guy with powerful friends is hard to corner.

A guy on his own has bills.

Somebody fixes the suit. Somebody makes the web fluid. Somebody buys the parts, patches the cuts and eats enough to keep a body like that running.

L isn't hunting Spider-Man's powers.

He's hunting Spider-Man's bills.

[PETER CUTAWAY: a tiny apartment in Queens. Radiator clanking. Peter's stitching a torn suit sleeve at the kitchen table, counting crumpled bills with his free hand.]

*Rent's due Friday. Web fluid's due… yesterday.*

Somewhere across the city, a stranger is building a profile of this exact kitchen table.

Peter has no idea.

Young. Seriously good with tech. Probably a chemist too. Less money than he used to have. Living close to where he patrols.

That's still tens of thousands of people.

So L asks a question nobody else chasing Spider-Man thinks to ask.

**When does Spider-Man choose NOT to help?**

---

# 2 — THE CRIMES SPIDER-MAN MISSES

Everybody else studies Spider-Man's rescues.

L studies the ones he skipped.

Two robberies, same distance away. Spider-Man stops one and skips the other.

Why?

A crash right near him, and the cops beat him there. Another way farther away, and he's on it almost instantly.

Why?

L comes up with a few theories.

Police radio? Public alerts? He just patrols and reacts to what he sees?

Or some kind of sense nobody understands?

Each theory predicts different behavior.

So L tests them.

No fake hostage situation. Nobody's life on the line.

With the authorities' help, he slips a few harmless fake alerts into different channels over several nights.

One only on a police channel.

One through public reports.

One only visible from a spot Spider-Man just swung past.

Spider-Man ignores the first one.

Shows up weirdly fast to the second.

Never goes near the third.

Then, during another test, something breaks every theory.

Spider-Man turns toward the scene before the alert could've even reached him.

L checks the timestamps.

Then checks them again.

He runs it again under different conditions.

Same weird result.

To L, precognition sounds ridiculous.

But he can't argue with the clock. Spider-Man knows things L can't see.

And that flips the case.

A tail might get spotted. An ambush might fail. A hidden watcher might tip him off just by being there.

L writes his second note:

**Spider-Man doesn't see the world the way I do.**

Peter's biggest advantage is that he can sense danger.

So L's plan is simple.

Stop being dangerous.

[PETER CUTAWAY: a rooftop. Peter, mask half-off, eating a cold slice of pizza. He pauses mid-bite and glances over his shoulder. Nothing there. He shrugs and keeps eating.]

---

# 3 — L BUILDS A CITY-SIZED EXPERIMENT

Instead of following Spider-Man, L studies what he leaves behind.

Decisions.

Where does he show up fast? Where does he show up late? Where does he vanish and never show up on another camera?

L's not looking for one route home. Peter would just switch it up.

He's looking for friction.

Some parts of the city are great for swinging. Some force awkward jumps. Some are private. Some are packed with cameras.

In the middle of the night, Spider-Man could be anywhere.

But the start and end of a patrol are where his real life leaks through.

The last sightings don't point to one building. Peter's too careful for that.

But they're not random either. They form a few wide corridors.

Now L has a hunting zone.

And then he does something you wouldn't expect.

He stops narrowing it down.

Narrow too fast and you start bending the evidence to fit your theory.

So he builds three competing guesses and waits.

One weakens. One holds. The third keeps looking better.

Then, overnight, Spider-Man's pattern falls apart.

His corridors stop predicting anything. His patrol times go all over the place. Routes that made perfect sense get dumped.

Either Spider-Man's life just flipped upside down…

or Spider-Man knows somebody's studying him.

And if it's the second one…

the game's already started.

---

# 4 — PETER REALIZES HE'S BEING MEASURED

Peter has no idea L exists.

But Peter Parker isn't dumb.

Spider-Sense doesn't hand him a memo. It just makes a few things feel off.

A police response that's a little too organized.

A harmless incident with way too many cameras pointed at all the right spots.

An alert that doesn't match what he finds when he gets there.

Once, you shrug it off.

Twice, you raise an eyebrow.

Three times, you start keeping score.

None of it's designed to hurt him.

And somehow that's the creepy part.

Villains want money. Revenge. Power.

This is different.

Somebody's asking questions.

How fast does Spider-Man show up? Where's he coming from? Where does he go after?

Nobody's attacking him.

Somebody's **measuring** him.

And every normal patrol is free intel.

So Peter changes.

Longer routes. Different times. Sometimes he changes clothes far from home and takes the train like everybody else.

And then he gets clever.

Four nights in a row, Spider-Man ends his patrol near the same part of the city.

It's not where Peter lives.

It's a decoy.

No weird turns. No looking over his shoulder. He just lets the pattern exist.

Then he waits.

Two nights later, Peter spots it.

A camera's been turned. Just a little. Just enough to cover an exit that used to be a blind spot.

Peter almost smiles.

Somebody took the bait.

But here's what Peter doesn't know…

L *wanted* him to notice.

If Spider-Man's smart enough to spot a profiler, sooner or later he'll test the profiler.

So L lets one camera move get spotted on purpose.

Now they both know the other one's thinking.

L isn't just studying Spider-Man anymore.

Spider-Man's studying L.

---

# 5 — THE FALSE NEIGHBORHOOD

Peter makes the first big move.

If the investigator wants geography, Peter's gonna give him geography.

Over the next week, Spider-Man quietly leans his movements toward a different neighborhood.

Not every night. Too obvious.

A little more often there at the start of patrols. A few more late-night disappearances pointing that way. Distant cameras catching him heading there after midnight.

He isn't trying to make it certain.

He's trying to make it convincing.

And it works.

L's model starts favoring the fake zone. He pulls profiles. Young. Good with tech. Not much money.

One person starts to stand out.

L lines up the guy's movements against Spider-Man's.

A match. Then another. Then another.

For the first time, L has a real person on the board.

And Peter put him there.

This is where a sloppier detective loses the case. Once you've got someone who looks right, every new fact starts looking like proof.

So L doesn't try to prove the guy *is* Spider-Man.

He tries to prove he **isn't**.

And he pulls it off. A confirmed Spider-Man sighting at a time this guy was provably somewhere else.

Suspect cleared.

Peter wins that round.

But L doesn't toss the failed theory.

He studies it.

Why did the wrong guy look so right?

The fake neighborhood showed up right after Spider-Man's behavior changed. And it's almost too clean. It's exactly what someone tracking him on a map would want to see.

The target didn't just hide.

He handed L a story.

Peter thinks he just sent the detective the wrong way.

Actually…

he just showed L how his mind works.

---

# 6 — THE INVESTIGATION TURNS INSIDE OUT

The map's been poisoned.

So L splits his data in two.

Everything **before** Spider-Man got suspicious goes in one pile. That's his real life.

Everything after goes in another. That's his game plan.

What did Spider-Man change right away?

Routes. Timing. Which calls he answers.

What *didn't* he change?

His values.

Peter can pick where he swings. He can even pick which camera sees him.

But changing his gut instinct to help people? Way harder.

L goes back through real emergencies already on record.

A suspect running one way. A hurt civilian the other way.

Spider-Man lets the suspect go.

Something dangerous he could lock down, or somebody who needs help.

He picks the person.

Again.

Again.

Again.

People's lives first. Catching the bad guy second. His own safety way down the list.

Any test built around greed or ego is weak. Spider-Man might just ignore it.

But responsibility?

That's different.

L could use that.

Before he can, though, a new problem shows up.

Peter's started looking for *him*.

---

# 7 — PETER HUNTS THE INVESTIGATOR

Cameras don't turn themselves.

Fake alerts don't write themselves.

Somewhere, someone's running this.

So Peter stops asking, "How are they following me?"

He starts asking, "Who's getting the data?"

He watches the cops after incidents instead of the criminals.

Which evidence gets picked up weirdly fast? Which cameras still matter after he's gone?

He follows the trail up the chain.

And it just… stops.

No detective on the case. No task-force boss. No face that keeps showing up.

Whoever's running this has basically erased themselves.

So Peter runs a test.

On one patrol, he lets a camera catch one of his web-shooters glitching out.

He switches wrists. Shakes the thing. Swings off.

It was never broken.

A few days later, a setup appears in a spot where losing one web-shooter would actually matter.

Peter has his answer.

The investigator bought the glitch and built a test around it.

They don't just collect evidence.

They design experiments with it.

But L learns something that same night.

Because when Spider-Man shows up…

the "broken" web-shooter works perfectly.

L stares at the footage.

The glitch was fake.

Both of them just ran experiments on each other…

at the same time.

And both experiments worked.

If L keeps testing Spider-Man directly, Peter will test the tests.

He needs a way in that Peter doesn't know exists.

So L goes backward.

Into Spider-Man's past.

---

# 8 — THE PERSON WHO SHOULD BE THERE

This is where Doctor Strange's spell gets interesting.

L has no clue magic wiped Peter Parker from everyone's memory.

He just notices Spider-Man's history has holes in it.

Spider-Man has years of public history. Stuff from when he was still in school. Huge battles.

So L asks a simple question.

If Spider-Man was this young back then…

where was his normal life while all of this was going on?

Picture a group photo where everyone is turned a little toward an empty spot.

One photo like that? Means nothing.

Hundreds of them, all turned toward the same empty spot?

Now you've got a story.

L finds events where the official explanation doesn't add up.

The same groups of people keep ending up near Spider-Man events.

Records explain what happened, but never quite how these people got involved.

The name "Peter Parker" is nowhere.

And that's the whole point.

L tries the normal explanations. A classified identity. Deleted records. Witness protection. A really good hack.

None of them explain why *people* act like the connection never existed.

L doesn't jump to magic.

He just calls it an unknown cause.

**Something happened that messed up the record of Spider-Man's real-life relationships.**

Then he asks when.

The holes in the history get worst right when Spider-Man's behavior changed.

Before: connected.

After: alone.

Before: a real life, tangled up with other people's lives.

After: almost nothing.

Somebody didn't just hide Spider-Man's identity.

Something cut it away.

And if L can find a real life that seems to *start* right at that break…

he finds the guy under the mask.

---

# 9 — PETER PARKER

L goes looking for people who fit the gap.

Right age. Right city. Seriously gifted at science. Not much money.

A real-life footprint that gets strangely thin right when Spider-Man goes solo.

And close to the clean map data from before Peter started faking things.

The list shrinks.

Then one name holds on longer than the rest.

Peter Parker.

On paper, Peter isn't Spider-Man.

He's barely anybody.

That's exactly what makes him interesting.

L isn't ready to call it. He sets up two possibilities.

Peter Parker *is* Spider-Man.

Or Peter Parker is *connected* to Spider-Man.

To rule one out, L does something Peter doesn't see coming.

He goes quiet.

No new traps. No camera changes. Nothing.

For a few days, Peter starts wondering if he finally lost whoever was on his tail.

Which is exactly what L wants.

The safest time to watch a careful target is when he thinks nobody's watching anymore.

Nobody tails Peter. Nobody parks outside his place.

Just timestamps from different places.

Peter shows up somewhere.

Later, Spider-Man shows up somewhere else.

Spider-Man disappears.

Peter's normal day picks back up.

Once, that's a coincidence.

Over and over, that's hard to ignore.

But there's never one clean switch. No moment where Peter walks into an alley and Spider-Man swings out.

L's confidence climbs.

His proof stays flat.

Then Peter realizes the silence is fake.

Someone this patient doesn't just quit.

The investigation didn't stop.

It went dark.

And if Peter can't see what L is measuring…

he can't mess up the measurements.

For the first time, Peter's actually behind.

---

# 10 — PETER'S BEST MOVE

Peter needs to kill the main theory.

Confusing it isn't enough.

If the investigator thinks Peter Parker is Spider-Man, there's one way to shut that down.

Show them both in two places at the same time.

Peter picks a time when a bunch of unrelated sources can confirm where he is.

Witnesses. Receipts. A normal reason to be somewhere for a good while.

Meanwhile, Spider-Man shows up somewhere else.

A stand-in. A setup. Something that makes a believable Spider-Man sighting happen.

For a few hours, L's best theory looks like it's falling apart.

Peter doesn't celebrate.

He waits.

A day. Then another.

Peter starts to think he might've actually won.

But L has a problem with the evidence.

It's too convenient.

For weeks, Peter Parker's records have been painfully boring. Then, right after the investigation goes quiet, a big clean contradiction shows up, aimed at the exact theory L was testing.

Why now?

So L doesn't ask if that Spider-Man *looked* real.

He asks if he *acted* real.

You can copy a costume. You can copy moves.

Faking decisions is a lot harder.

The Spider-Man in that sighting never has to make a real choice. No second threat. No random civilian in trouble.

Nothing that tests the priorities L has spent weeks tracking.

It proves someone *looked* like Spider-Man.

It doesn't prove Spider-Man was there.

So L gets more sure, not less.

Peter didn't hand him an alibi.

He handed him an alibi built for this exact investigation.

L was never asking, "Can Peter and Spider-Man be in two places?"

He was asking, "What will Peter do if he thinks I suspect him?"

And Peter answered.

---

# 11 — THE CASE L CANNOT PROVE

Now L believes Peter is Spider-Man.

But believing isn't proving.

Imagine L laying out his case.

Peter's young.

So are millions of people.

Peter's brilliant.

Last time I checked, that's legal.

His schedule lines up with Spider-Man's.

Correlation.

His history is weird.

Still no proof.

He came up with a suspicious alibi.

Still no proof.

How do you explain to anyone that the world's memory has a Peter-Parker-shaped hole in it, when nobody remembers what used to be there?

L has solved the mystery…

but he can't prove it.

And Peter gets the difference.

So he locks in.

He never changes where he could be seen. Never keeps the suit anywhere obvious. Never goes straight from Peter to Spider-Man.

And for the first time, L starts running out of new evidence.

L could confront Peter. But Peter knows reactions are evidence.

More surveillance? Spider-Sense.

Another staged emergency? Peter's expecting it.

So L needs one last test.

A trap for Peter Parker, not Spider-Man.

Peter can't know he's being tested.

Spider-Sense can't point him to the watcher.

And it has to make Peter choose between protecting his secret…

and the one rule L knows he almost never breaks.

Responsibility.

This is where it stops being a forensics problem.

It becomes a mind game.

---

# 12 — L'S FINAL EXPERIMENT

L won't stage a real disaster.

He controls what Peter hears instead.

Peter gets a believable reason to think a preventable accident is about to happen nearby.

Peter. Not Spider-Man.

It reaches him through something totally normal in his everyday life.

And L doesn't put a single watcher on Peter.

If Spider-Sense picks up hostile attention, L makes sure there isn't any.

The surroundings do the watching.

Something's about to fail. Something that looks like it could turn deadly in minutes.

And supposedly, nobody's called 911 yet.

Peter has a choice.

Walk away and protect the secret.

Or do something.

But Peter has learned.

His first thought isn't the emergency.

It's the investigation.

Is this another test?

Peter waits.

The old Peter would already be running.

L saw the hesitation coming.

Peter checks for any sign he's being watched.

He can't find one.

His Spider-Sense isn't screaming.

Maybe it's real.

Maybe it's not.

L isn't forcing Peter to reveal himself.

He's forcing Peter to decide without knowing.

Seconds go by.

Peter can't take the risk.

But he doesn't suit up.

He goes as Peter Parker.

He warns people. Hits a safety switch. Gives everyone a reason to move away.

No powers. Nothing anyone could point to.

He saves people…

and keeps Spider-Man out of it.

For a second, it looks like Peter beat the trap.

Then L goes back over what happened.

Peter Parker reacted to a threat he had no reason to think was serious.

Could be a coincidence.

Except L can run the same setup again.

Different emergency. Different place. Different way of getting the word to him.

The one thing that stays the same is Peter.

And Peter sees the danger right away.

If he keeps reacting, L gets a pattern.

If he suddenly stops, that goes against everything Spider-Man has shown for years.

L has finally built a test Peter can't beat by changing his route.

Because it isn't about where Peter goes.

It's about who Peter is.

It still won't hold up in court.

But L knows.

Peter knows L knows.

And neither of them has the one thing that ends the game.

So Peter does something L doesn't see coming.

He stops hiding from the investigator.

And goes looking for a conversation.

---

# 13 — THE MEETING

Peter's spent weeks piecing together the invisible hand behind all this.

He never gets a name.

But he knows the setup well enough to figure out where everything leads.

At some point, whether L plans it or not, the last bit of distance closes.

Peter walks into a quiet public place.

No costume. Just Peter Parker.

L's already there.

Peter's Spider-Sense stays calm.

L isn't there to hurt him.

Peter sits down.

Neither of them has to say "Spider-Man."

That's the game.

L starts with something harmless. School. Then science. Then New York.

Then that weird habit Peter has of ending up near emergencies.

Peter gives answers that make sense.

L lets them slide.

He brings up a fake neighborhood.

Peter's face barely moves.

A web-shooter glitch that wasn't real.

Blank.

An alibi.

Peter stays locked in.

L isn't after a confession.

He's watching for the questions Peter never asks.

Someone innocent would've said something by now.

Why are you telling me this?

What does Spider-Man have to do with me?

Peter doesn't say a word.

Because he already knows how every one of these topics connects.

And L knows he knows.

Finally, Peter says it.

"You can't prove it."

L doesn't answer right away.

Because Peter's right.

L can explain every pattern. Every change. Every counter-move.

But unless Peter suits up on camera, leaves something behind that could only be his, or confesses…

there's still a gap between *knowing* and *proving*.

Peter found the one place he can still hold his ground against L.

Doubt.

L can win the investigation…

and still lose the case.

---

# 14 — THE DARKER VERSION

But there's one thing that should scare Peter more than any of this.

We gave him the chance to realize someone was investigating him.

That's the only reason he could fight back.

Take that away…

and this case gets a lot darker.

Imagine L never makes an obvious move.

Never turns a visible camera.

Never feeds Peter a suspicious emergency.

Peter keeps patrolling like normal.

Normal routes.

Normal habits.

Normal mistakes.

For thirty days straight.

L gets the schedule. The travel patterns. The profile. The weird gap in the records.

Peter Parker ends up on the suspect list…

and Peter doesn't even know he needs an alibi.

---

# 15 — THE VERDICT

So, could L catch Spider-Man?

Could he figure out who's under the mask?

Yes. Spider-Man gives away way too much just by how he acts.

Could Peter fight back?

Absolutely. Fake neighborhood. Fake glitch. Fake alibi.

But every lie is another decision.

And decisions show you the mind making them.

So L gets a strange kind of win.

He solves Spider-Man.

But he can't expose him.

And Peter gets his own strange kind of win.

He keeps the secret…

but he can never again act like nobody's watching.

Because Peter's real protection after *No Way Home* isn't that the world forgot him.

It's the gap between **knowing something**…

and **proving it**.

So here's the question.

If L already knows Peter Parker is Spider-Man…

**how does he prove it without Peter ever figuring out what he's doing?**

That's the case I want you to solve.
