# LOGIC NEXUS — MASTER SCRIPT (v2)
## **Could L Catch Spider-Man?**

**Continuity:** MCU Peter Parker, a few weeks after *No Way Home*. January in New York.
**Narration starts at 0:00. No intro. No logo. No music-only lead-in.**
**Target length:** ~4,300 words, about 27–29 minutes of voice-over.

**Recurring on-screen devices:**
- **L'S NOTES #1–#7.** A handwritten card appears each time L writes a rule. Keep the numbering visible.
- **SCOREBOARD.** A small "L 0 – PETER 0" tally in the corner. It updates at every round.
- **Voice split.** L sections are slow, cold and precise. Peter sections are warmer and rougher, with his inner voice in *italics*.

---

### 0:00 — COLD OPEN

[ON SCREEN: grainy traffic-cam footage. Night. Spider-Man lands on a ledge.]

January. 2:14 a.m. A traffic camera in Midtown.

Spider-Man lands on a ledge… and his left web-shooter jams.

He shakes it. Switches wrists. Swings off one-handed.

Nothing was broken.

Peter faked it.

Because he'd figured out somebody was watching him.

And the person watching?

Already knew it was fake.

[HARD CUT. Black screen. One sound hit.]

### 0:18 — HOOK

The whole world forgot Peter Parker.

L doesn't need them to remember.

Because every night Spider-Man puts on the mask, he makes the same mistake.

He acts like Peter Parker.

He picks which crimes to stop. He goes home at roughly the same time. He wears a suit somebody had to sew by hand.

And L has caught people with a lot less.

But this time the target has something L has never faced.

Spider-Sense.

A warning system that goes off before Peter even knows why.

You can't tail a man like that. You can't ambush him. And the moment he figures out he's being studied, every pattern L finds becomes a pattern Peter can fake.

So this doesn't stay an investigation for long.

It turns into a chess match between the world's greatest detective…

and a kid who's already lost everything once, and isn't about to lose the last thing he has left.

His secret.

And somewhere in this case, Peter makes one mistake.

It isn't the one you're expecting.

### 0:58 — THE RULES

Quick ground rules, so nobody's yelling in the comments.

One. L gets hired by Damage Control. After the Statue of Liberty, they're sitting on a file with a masked vigilante and no name. So L gets police data, city cameras and public records. No Avengers files. No Stark tech.

Two. Doctor Strange's spell didn't just wipe people's memories. It wiped every record that ties Peter Parker to Spider-Man. That includes Mysterio's broadcast. It still exists, but now it's a dead man pointing at a mask and no name comes out.

Otherwise, this video is four seconds long.

Three. Everything else survives. Every sighting. Every receipt. Every camera. Every person who's still alive… and still acting a little strange.

Could L catch Spider-Man?

Let's give him the case.

[SCOREBOARD appears: L 0 – PETER 0]

## 1:30 — CHAPTER 1: THE RHYTHM

L doesn't start by looking for Spider-Man.

That's the mistake everybody else makes.

Search eight million people for a guy in a mask and you get eight million suspects.

So L starts somewhere way more boring.

Time.

He pulls every confirmed Spider-Man sighting from the last three months. Police reports. News helicopters. Traffic cams. Dashcams. Anything with a timestamp he can actually check.

Then he throws most of it out.

Blurry TikToks. Gone.

"I think I saw him" posts. Gone.

Guys in Party City costumes. Gone.

*(Remember that last one. It comes back.)*

L doesn't want more data. He wants data he can trust.

And when he lines up what's left, something jumps out.

Spider-Man isn't always available.

There are gaps. Big ones.

Which is weird, when you think about it. This guy crosses Manhattan faster than a cab. He can hear a car alarm from six blocks away. He can go all night.

So why does he vanish?

L breaks the week into hours. Weekdays against weekends. Real emergencies against routine patrols.

And the picture sharpens.

Big disasters? Spider-Man shows up whenever.

Routine patrols? Those keep a schedule.

Emergencies show where he *can* go.

Patrols show where he *chooses* to go.

So the gaps aren't about ability.

They're about life.

He sleeps. He eats. Maybe he works. Maybe he studies.

L writes his first note.

[L'S NOTES #1: **The man under the mask controls the schedule.**]

Then he immediately tries to prove himself wrong.

Does the pattern follow weather? Police shift changes? Crime rates?

A little.

Not enough.

The human schedule is still there.

So L has something. Not a name. Not a face.

A heartbeat.

And people can hide their faces a lot more easily than the rhythm of their lives.

But then L notices something else.

The rhythm changed.

A few weeks ago, Spider-Man's whole pattern broke and reset.

Like his life ended… and started over.

---

## 3:40 — CHAPTER 2: WHAT IT COSTS TO BE SPIDER-MAN

L puts the old Spider-Man next to the new one.

The old one had a nanotech suit. Showed up next to Avengers. Took selfies. Had money behind him, and probably people.

The new one?

Quieter. More local. Alone.

And the suit… L zooms in on a frame from a bodega camera.

Those seams are hand-stitched.

L doesn't care *why* yet. He treats it like a supply-chain problem.

Somebody cut this guy off.

And that's great news for L.

Because a man with a billionaire behind him has no weak spots.

A man on his own has bills.

Somebody has to fix that suit. Somebody has to mix the web fluid. Somebody has to buy the chemicals, patch the cuts and eat about five thousand calories a day to swing across Queens.

L writes it down.

[L'S NOTES #2: **Don't hunt his powers. Hunt his expenses.**]

Now the profile starts coming together.

Young. You can hear it in the jokes.

A gifted engineer. Probably a chemist too.

Broke. At least compared to before.

And living close to where his patrols start and end, because swinging home in January will kill you.

[CUT TO: Peter. Tiny apartment. Radiator clanking. He's sewing a torn suit sleeve with dental floss.]

*Okay. Okay. Twelve bucks left for the week, and the good thread's gone, so… dental floss. Mint flavored. At least the suit'll smell nice.*

He hasn't said his own name out loud to anyone in three weeks.

There's no one left to say it to.

Profile's still too broad, though. Tens of thousands of people fit it.

So L asks the question nobody else would.

**When does Spider-Man choose *not* to help?**

---

## 6:10 — CHAPTER 3: THE CRIMES HE MISSES

Every other investigator studies Spider-Man's rescues.

L studies the ones he missed.

Two muggings, the same distance away. He shows up at one and skips the other.

Why?

A car wreck nearby: he never shows. A fire across the borough: he's there in ninety seconds.

Why?

So L builds theories about how Spider-Man finds trouble.

Police scanner? Public 911 feeds? Just looking around? Tips from civilians?

Each theory predicts different behavior. So L tests them.

No fake hostages. Nobody in danger.

Just information.

Working with Damage Control, he slips harmless fake alerts into different channels over several nights.

One only on a police frequency.

One on public news apps.

One that you could only see from a rooftop Spider-Man had just crossed.

The results come back.

Police frequency? Spider-Man is there almost instantly.

So Peter's running a police scanner. Classic.

News apps? He ignores it.

The rooftop one? He never comes.

Clean. Easy. Case closed on that question.

Then, on the fifth night, something happens that doesn't fit any theory at all.

Spider-Man changes direction before the alert even goes out.

L checks the timestamps.

He checks them again.

He's early. By almost two full seconds.

L runs it again. Different street. Different night.

Early again.

L doesn't believe in precognition. That's not how he's built.

But he can't argue with the clock. Spider-Man knows things before any channel could tell him.

[L'S NOTES #3: **He knows before he should know. Don't assume he sees what I see.**]

And that changes everything.

A tail might get noticed. An ambush might fail. An agent on a rooftop might tip him off just by being tense.

Peter's biggest advantage is that he can sense danger.

So L's answer is simple.

Stop being dangerous.

---

## 8:45 — CHAPTER 4: PETER GETS MEASURED

L stops chasing Spider-Man and starts studying what he leaves behind.

Not webs. Not footprints.

Decisions.

Where does he show up fast? Where does he come from street level instead of from above? Where does he vanish in the one spot no camera covers?

L isn't hunting for one route home. Peter would just change it.

He's hunting for friction.

Midtown's perfect for swinging. Queens flattens out. Some blocks are packed with cameras. Some are blind.

A patrol can go anywhere at 2 a.m.

But the first sighting of the night, and the last one, those touch real life.

The last sightings don't point to one building. Peter's not that sloppy.

But they don't scatter randomly either. They bunch into three corridors. All three run into Queens.

L gives himself three competing theories and waits.

One weakens. One holds.

And the third one keeps getting stronger.

Then, overnight, the whole pattern falls apart.

[CUT TO: Peter on a water tower, rain dripping off the mask.]

Because Peter's noticed something.

His Spider-Sense isn't screaming. It's more like… humming. Like a wrong note.

A police response that's a little *too* organized.

A car break-in with way too many cameras pointed at it.

An alert on his scanner that doesn't match what he finds when he gets there.

Once is noise.

Twice is weird.

Three times is a pattern.

*Nobody's shooting at me. Nobody's asking for money. Nobody's monologuing, which, honestly, I kind of miss.*

*Somebody's just… taking notes.*

And that's when it hits him.

He's not being attacked.

He's being **measured**.

Which means every normal night he patrols gives away free information.

So Peter does exactly what L was afraid of.

He changes.

Longer routes. Different times. Sometimes he takes the 7 train in a hoodie, just another tired guy with a backpack.

And then he gets clever.

Four nights in a row, he ends his patrol near the same block in Astoria.

Not his block.

A decoy.

And he watches that block to see if anything changes.

Two nights later, it does.

A camera on a light pole has been turned. Only about ten degrees. Just enough to cover an alley that used to be blind.

*Gotcha.*

Peter almost laughs out loud.

Somebody's out there. And for about four seconds, alone on a rooftop, it feels like company.

He hasn't had that since Christmas.

But here's what Peter doesn't know.

L *wanted* him to see that camera.

Because L had worked out the same thing from the other direction. A target smart enough to notice he's being profiled is smart enough to test the profiler.

So L let him win one.

Now both of them know the other one is thinking.

[SCOREBOARD: L 1 – PETER 1]

From here on, L isn't just studying Spider-Man.

Spider-Man is studying L.

---

## 12:00 — CHAPTER 5: THE FALSE NEIGHBORHOOD

Peter makes the first real move.

*You want geography? Fine. I'll give you geography.*

For a whole week, he leans his patrols toward a neighborhood in Jackson Heights.

Not every night. That would look fake.

He builds the pattern the way statistics build: a nudge here, a nudge there.

His first sighting of the night lands nearby a little more often. His last disappearances drift that way. He lets a far-off camera catch him heading there after midnight.

He isn't trying to make it certain.

He's trying to make it convincing.

And it works.

L's model starts favoring Jackson Heights. He starts pulling profiles. Young. Technical. Broke. Irregular hours.

One name floats up. A nineteen-year-old engineering dropout who works nights at a repair shop.

The fit isn't perfect. Real cases never are.

L lines up the kid's movements against Spider-Man's.

One match. Then another. Then another.

For the first time, there's a real face on the board.

And Peter put it there.

This is where a lesser detective loses the case. Once you've got a suspect who feels right, every new fact looks like proof.

L knows that trap.

So he doesn't try to prove the kid is Spider-Man.

He tries to prove he *isn't*.

And he does. There's a confirmed Spider-Man sighting in the Bronx while the kid is on camera, clocked in, fixing a laptop.

Suspect cleared.

[SCOREBOARD: L 1 – PETER 2]

Peter wins the round.

But L doesn't throw the failed theory away.

He studies it.

Why did the wrong answer look *so* good?

The false neighborhood only showed up *after* Spider-Man's behavior changed. And it's too clean. It's exactly the kind of evidence a geographic profiler dreams about.

He didn't just hide information.

He *fed* it.

L pulls his knees up to his chest and leans toward the screen.

Peter thinks he just sent the detective the wrong way.

What he actually did was show L how his mind works.

[L'S NOTES #4: **Every lie is a decision. Decisions have fingerprints.**]

---

## 15:00 — CHAPTER 6: TWO EXPERIMENTS, ONE NIGHT

L splits his data in half.

Everything before Peter noticed goes in one pile. That's his real life.

Everything after goes in another. That's his strategy.

Then L asks what Peter changed, and what he didn't.

He changed routes. Timing. Which calls he answers.

What didn't he change?

His conscience.

L goes through months of real footage. A fleeing suspect one way, a hurt civilian the other way: Spider-Man lets the suspect go. A crate of stolen guns, or a kid stuck on a fire escape: he goes for the kid.

Every time.

Human life first. The bad guy second. Himself? Somewhere way down the list.

[L'S NOTES #5: **He can fake a route. He can't fake who he saves.**]

L files that away. He'll need it.

But first, Peter hits back.

Because Peter's been hunting too. Not L, since he doesn't know L exists yet. He's hunting whoever is behind the cameras.

Cameras don't turn themselves. Fake alerts don't write themselves. Somebody's giving orders.

So Peter watches the cops instead of the criminals. Which evidence gets bagged way too fast? Which cameras still matter after he's gone?

He follows the chain upward, and it just… stops. No lead detective. No task force. No name.

*Okay, so it's not a cop. Cops have names. Cops have terrible mustaches. This is… something else.*

So Peter runs a test.

That's the cold open. The jammed web-shooter, the wrist switch, the one-handed swing on a traffic cam at 2:14 a.m.

Nothing was broken.

Then Peter waits to see if anyone takes the bait.

Nine days later, one of those "suspicious" alerts comes in from a spot where you'd *need* both shooters to get out safely.

*There it is.* The investigator saw the footage, believed it, and designed a test around it.

Now Peter knows how his opponent works. This person doesn't just collect evidence. They build experiments out of it.

But L learns something that same night.

Because Spider-Man shows up, and both shooters work perfectly.

L plays the footage twice.

The malfunction was fake. Peter fed him false information just to see if the investigation would react.

Two people, running experiments on each other, on the same night.

And both experiments worked.

[SCOREBOARD: L 2 – PETER 3]

That's when L realizes that if he keeps testing Spider-Man directly, Peter will test the tests.

He needs a path Peter doesn't know exists.

So L goes backward.

Not into Spider-Man's present.

Into his past.

---

## 18:10 — CHAPTER 7: THE HOLE IN THE PHOTOGRAPH

This is where Doctor Strange's spell gets interesting.

L doesn't know about magic. He doesn't even know what he's looking for.

He just knows Spider-Man didn't appear out of nowhere. He has *years* of history. The Washington Monument. The Europe trip. The Statue of Liberty.

And if Spider-Man was this young back then…

where was his normal life while all this was happening?

So L rebuilds the history, event by event. Not looking for Spider-Man.

Looking for who's standing next to him.

Imagine a group photo where everybody is turned slightly toward an empty spot.

One photo? Means nothing.

Hundreds of them, all turned toward the same empty spot?

That's a person-shaped hole.

And the same names keep turning up around that hole.

Michelle Jones. Ned Leeds.

In the elevator at the Washington Monument. On the school trip when London and Venice got torn up. On the scaffolding at the Statue of Liberty.

L even reads a memoir by a Midtown High kid named Flash Thompson, who claims he was Spider-Man's best friend. It's… a lot. But MJ and Ned are all over it.

So L sends people to talk to them. They pose as journalists doing a one-year-later story on the Statue of Liberty.

MJ remembers everything. The explosions. The cold. The scaffolding.

But ask her *why* she was there, or who brought her, and the answers go flat. Like reading a script that's missing a page.

Ned's the same. He remembers the night. Not the reason.

They remember the events, but not the person who connects them.

L has never seen anything like it.

He tries the normal explanations first. Witness protection. Deleted records. A government cover-up. A hack.

None of them explain why *people* act like the connection never existed.

So he doesn't use the word magic. He doesn't need to.

He calls it an unknown mechanism, and keeps going.

[L'S NOTES #6: **Something erased the person. It couldn't erase the shape he left behind.**]

Then L goes back to his old data. The clean pile, from before Peter knew he was being watched.

He filters out the rescues and the fights. He only looks at the quiet moments: the times Spider-Man stops and sits still.

And one spot lights up.

A water tower in Queens, across the street from a coffee shop.

The coffee shop where Michelle Jones works.

---

## 21:00 — CHAPTER 8: THE REGULAR

L doesn't send a SWAT team.

He sends one quiet observer with a laptop, to sit in the corner of that coffee shop for a week.

He isn't told to look for a superhero.

He's told to look for a ghost.

Someone who comes in a lot. Pays in cash. Looks at the girl behind the counter like he's lost something.

It takes four days.

A kid. Early twenties, maybe younger. Black coffee. Exact change. Always the same seat, the one with a view of the counter and the door.

A tray of mugs crashes in the kitchen, and everybody jumps.

Except him.

He'd already turned his head *before* it hit the floor.

L runs the name.

Peter Parker.

And this is where it gets eerie. Peter Parker basically doesn't exist before this winter. He has a new GED application. A tiny apartment lease that started the same week the world forgot. No transcripts. No old addresses. No history anybody can find.

A genius-level kid whose life begins on the exact week Spider-Man lost everything.

L's confidence jumps.

His proof doesn't.

Because there's no footage of Peter walking into an alley and Spider-Man walking out. No suit in his trash. Nothing.

So L does something Peter doesn't expect.

He stops.

No more fake alerts. No turned cameras. Nothing.

A few days go by, and Peter starts to wonder if it's over.

That's exactly what L wants. The safest time to watch a careful target is when he thinks nobody's watching anymore.

But Peter's good. Maybe too good.

Because after a week of silence, he realizes that the silence *is* the move.

*Someone this patient doesn't quit. They go dark.*

And if Peter can't see what's being measured…

he can't fake the measurements.

For the first time in the whole case, Peter's behind.

---

## 23:10 — CHAPTER 9: THE PERFECT ALIBI

So Peter swings big.

If someone suspects Peter Parker is Spider-Man, there's one clean way to kill that idea.

Put them in two places at once.

Peter picks a Friday night. He's at a packed study group at the Queens library for three hours. People who'll remember him. A card swipe. A café receipt.

And at 9:40 p.m., Spider-Man shows up in Brooklyn and stops a purse-snatcher, on three phones and a news camera.

How?

Remember those Party City costumes L threw out in Chapter One?

Peter found the best Spider-Man cosplayer in New York. Paid him forty bucks and a pizza. Told him to jump off a dumpster, web up a "thief" (his cousin), wave, and leave.

It's perfect.

For about six hours, L's best theory falls apart.

And Peter doesn't celebrate. He waits.

A day. Two days.

Nothing.

*Did it work? I think it worked. Please tell me it worked.*

It didn't work.

Because L has one big problem with this alibi.

It's too good.

For weeks, Peter Parker's life has been painfully boring. And then, right after L goes quiet, a perfect, airtight contradiction shows up. Aimed at the exact theory L was testing.

So L doesn't ask whether that Spider-Man *looked* real.

He asks whether he *behaved* real.

Anybody can copy a costume. You can copy the moves.

Making the choices is harder.

The Brooklyn Spider-Man rescues a guy with a fake purse, and doesn't even glance at the old woman who slips on the ice ten feet away.

The real Spider-Man has never walked past someone who fell. Not once, in hundreds of hours of footage.

[L'S NOTES #5 flashes back on screen: **He can't fake who he saves.**]

That wasn't Spider-Man.

That was a guy in a costume.

So Peter didn't produce an alibi.

He produced a reaction. Exactly the reaction of someone who knew *what* he was suspected of.

The question L was asking was never "can Peter and Spider-Man be in different places?"

It was "what does Peter do when he thinks I'm onto him?"

And Peter answered.

[SCOREBOARD: L 3 – PETER 3]

---

## 25:30 — CHAPTER 10: THE TRAP

Now it's tied, and L has run out of normal moves.

Confront Peter? That's just another reaction, and Peter knows it.

More surveillance? Spider-Sense.

Another fake emergency? Peter's expecting it.

So L builds a trap that doesn't test where Peter goes.

It tests who he is.

And this is the part of the case that should make you uncomfortable.

L doesn't gamble with lives.

He gambles with feelings.

Tuesday. Closing time at the coffee shop. It's raining.

Across the street, a contractor's scaffold has been going up for a week. L arranged that. It's rigged with hidden safety cables, so nobody can actually get hurt.

At 9:02 p.m., a bolt gives. A metal pole tilts over the sidewalk.

Right where MJ is taking out the trash.

Now think about what that means.

The danger isn't real. So Peter's Spider-Sense stays completely quiet.

He's sitting in his usual seat. Nothing is telling him to move.

Nothing except his own eyes.

And his eyes see *her*.

He's across the room, and then he isn't. Out the door. One arm around her shoulders. He pulls her back a step and a half.

The pole swings and stops. Right where the cables catch it.

Nobody's hurt. Nothing happened.

MJ looks up at him. Rain on her face. A total stranger who got there way too fast.

"…Thanks," she says.

"Yeah," Peter says. "Just… be careful, okay?"

She nods, the way you nod at a nice stranger.

She has no idea who he is.

[BEAT. Let the music drop out.]

The shop's security camera recorded the whole thing. The frame count puts his reaction at about a tenth of a second. That's faster than any human can react to something they see.

Peter didn't use a single power anyone could point to.

He didn't need to.

His whole case was about hiding Spider-Man.

And the one thing he couldn't hide was that he still loves her.

That's the mistake I promised you at the start. It wasn't a route. It wasn't the alibi.

It was a coffee shop.

Because Spider-Sense protects Peter from danger.

Nothing protects him from her.

[L'S NOTES #7: **The spell erased her memory of him. It didn't erase his memory of her.**]

[SCOREBOARD: L 4 – PETER 3]

## 27:10 — CHAPTER 11: THE BOOTH

Two days later, Peter walks into the coffee shop and heads for his seat.

Someone's already in it.

Crouched on the chair, not sitting. Barefoot in January. Stacking sugar cubes into a little tower beside a cup of tea.

Peter's Spider-Sense doesn't go off.

Because the man in his seat isn't trying to hurt him.

"You're in my seat," Peter says.

"I know," L says. "It has the best view of the counter."

Peter sits down across from him.

Neither of them says "Spider-Man." That's the game.

L asks about the GED. Then chemistry. Then New York. Then, almost bored, about "the scaffold accident the other night."

Peter gives normal answers.

L tells him about a neighborhood in Jackson Heights that turned out to be fake.

Peter's face doesn't move.

A web-shooter that wasn't really broken.

Nothing.

A cosplayer in Brooklyn who walked right past an old woman on the ice.

Still nothing.

But L isn't watching Peter's face.

He's watching what Peter *doesn't* ask.

An innocent guy would have said "why are you telling me this?" twenty minutes ago. Or "who are you?" Or at least "what does any of this have to do with me?"

Peter never asks.

Because he already knows how every story connects.

And L knows he knows.

Finally, Peter leans in.

"You can't prove any of this."

L drops one more sugar cube in his tea.

"No," L says. "I'm ninety-seven percent certain. Which, in a courtroom, is worth nothing."

He stands to leave. Then he stops.

"For what it's worth…"

And he says it, quietly, the way you'd say an old friend's name.

"…Peter."

Peter doesn't move.

Because nobody has said his name like that in weeks.

Like they *know* who he is.

[SCOREBOARD fades.]

## 28:30 — THE VERDICT

So. Could L catch Spider-Man?

Here's my answer.

**L identifies him in about three weeks. Proves it? Never.**

He can work out the rhythm, the expenses, the neighborhood, the history and the name. He can even predict the choices Peter will make.

But catching Spider-Man isn't like catching Kira. There's no body. No murder weapon. Just a kid who swings a little too fast and cares a little too much.

Peter wins the courtroom.

L wins the truth.

But here's the part that stays with me.

The whole world forgot Peter Parker. His best friend. The girl he loves. Everyone.

And after all that…

the only person on Earth who knows his name…

is the one who was hunting him.

In this version, Peter noticed. The turned camera. The fake alerts. He got to fight back.

Now imagine L never makes a single visible move.

Thirty days of silence. Peter patrols like normal. Normal routes. Normal habits. Normal mistakes.

He never builds a fake neighborhood. Never hires a cosplayer.

He never even finds out he needs an alibi.

That version doesn't end with tea in a coffee shop.
