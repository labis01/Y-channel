# WRITER'S BRIEF: *Could L Catch Spider-Man?* (v3)

**Channel:** Logic Nexus  **Audience:** United States, 18–40, sharp and detail-focused
**Target runtime:** 26–30 min (~4,300 spoken words)
**This brief replaces v2.** Keep v2's investigative backbone. The changes are in stakes, the villain, the emotional arc and the originality layer.

---

## 1. Logline

Three weeks after the world forgot Peter Parker, a federal agency hires the world's most famous detective to put a name to the last masked vigilante in New York.

L can find him. That was never really the question.

The question is what the man who hired him will do with the name.

---

## 2. Why this version is different (use these in the video and in the description)

Most "Could X catch Y" videos end at *can he*. This one turns on *should he*.

1. **An original antagonist.** Deputy Director Graham Stroud is our own character, not borrowed from Marvel or Death Note. He gives the story its villain and gives L a moral turn.
2. **Real-world method.** Every step L takes uses a real New York or U.S. system: NYPD's Domain Awareness System, Real-Time Crime Center, NYC Open Data, 311 complaints, MTA OMNY tap data, frame-rate forensics, geographic profiling. A skeptical viewer should be able to check it.
3. **A two-sided hunt.** Peter isn't prey. He counter-profiles the investigation and wins rounds on the scoreboard.
4. **One emotional thesis.** The whole world forgot Peter Parker, and the only person who learns his name again is the one hunting him.
5. **Viewers take part.** The ending hands the audience a real problem to solve, and the best answer shapes Part 2.

---

## 3. Characters

### Peter Parker: the hunted (the audience's heart)
- **Where he is:** 18. It's January, three weeks after the spell. May is dead. MJ and Ned don't know him. Happy stood at May's grave and didn't recognize him. He has a studio apartment in Queens, a GED book, a police scanner on the radiator and a suit he sewed himself.
- **Surface:** jokes, sarcasm, pop-culture asides. Humor is how he copes, and the writer should use it that way, not just as filler.
- **Underneath:** he hasn't said his own name to anyone in weeks. Part of him *wants* to be seen.
- **Arc:** hiding → outsmarting → realizing he can't stop caring (MJ) → being *known* by one person, which is terrifying and a relief at the same time.
- **Rule:** at least one line of Peter's inner voice in every Peter section. Include one small, specific detail of being broke in each act (dental floss for thread, $12 for the week, walking to save a fare).

### L: the hunter (the audience's mind, then its conscience)
- **Canon tics, used sparingly:** crouching, bare feet, sugar cubes, stating certainty in percentages, speaking flat. Watari is a voice on the phone.
- **Surface:** cold. At first Peter is only a puzzle to him.
- **Underneath (the original angle):** L is an orphan too. He knows what it's like to be nobody on paper. He never says it outright. Show it in one moment where he pauses too long.
- **Arc:** "I will find him" → "I have found him" → "I will not hand him over." His turn has to be *earned* by Stroud's actions, not by a speech.
- **Rule:** L must stay brilliant and never be sentimental. His final choice should come across as logic: *the client has become more dangerous than the target.*

### Deputy Director Graham Stroud: the antagonist (**built to be disliked**)
- **Who he is:** a deputy director in the U.S. Department of Damage Control (DODC). Mid-50s. Wears a lanyard as if it were a badge of honor. Original character.
- **What he wants:** the Statue of Liberty is on Liberty Island, which is federal property (a National Park Service site). The damage there gives him a legal pretext: federal charges against "the masked individual." What he actually wants is *leverage*: a name he can hold over Spider-Man to turn him into a government asset, and a public arrest to justify his budget.
- **Why the audience will hate him.** Show it through specific actions, not by the narrator calling him evil:
  1. He calls Peter "the asset" and MJ "the barista." He never uses their names.
  2. At the midpoint he wants to arrest the **innocent** 19-year-old repair-shop kid (Peter's false-trail suspect) "because the public needs a face by Friday." L has to prove the kid innocent *against* Stroud's wishes.
  3. He takes credit for L's work in a briefing.
  4. **The unforgivable act:** without telling L, he removes the safety cables from L's controlled test. The danger to MJ becomes *real*.
- **Voice:** calm, reasonable-sounding, bureaucratic. American viewers already distrust official overreach and surveillance used for power. Tap that feeling. Don't make him a cartoon.
- **Line to build around:** *"I don't need him understood. I need him owned."*

### Supporting
- **MJ:** one real scene (the rescue) and a small moment of déjà vu. She says *"…Have we met?"* and Peter says *"No. I'd remember."*
- **Ned:** one scene, the interview where he can't explain why he was at the Statue of Liberty.
- **The repair-shop kid (original):** Marco, 19, working nights to pay his mom's rent. He's a human face for what Stroud is willing to do to an innocent person.
- **Times Square Spider-Man (original):** Dre, a costumed character who poses for tips. He's Peter's alibi decoy. **This replaces the v2 Party City line; Party City closed all of its stores in 2025, and viewers will notice.**

## 5. Realism rules (for a detail-focused U.S. audience)

Get these right. Commenters will check.

- **Real systems, used correctly:** NYPD Domain Awareness System and Real-Time Crime Center (camera and data fusion); NYC Open Data and 311 complaint logs; MTA **OMNY** taps (MetroCard is being phased out); the Freedom of Information Law (FOIL) as a joke line only.
- **Geography:** the 7 train runs through Sunnyside, Woodside and Jackson Heights. Astoria is on the N/W. Queens gets lower, which makes web-swinging harder there. January weather matters (ice, wind, early dark).
- **Law:** Liberty Island is federal property, so federal charges are plausible. "Being Spider-Man" isn't a crime by itself; Stroud needs the Statue of Liberty damage as his pretext.
- **Science:** 30 fps means about 33 ms per frame. Human visual reaction is typically about 200–250 ms. World Athletics treats a start under 100 ms as a false start. Say the math out loud once; it's a strong credibility moment.
- **Canon accuracy:** the spell happened around Christmas and the story is set in January. *Flashpoint* is Flash's book. Happy at May's grave is from the end of the film. **Fix from v2:** the interviews are a "feature on the Statue of Liberty incident," not a "one-year-later story" (it's only been weeks).
- **L never does anything a real investigator would call impossible.** When he guesses, he says so and gives a percentage.

## 6. Voice and style rules

- **L sections:** slow, short, precise, cold. **Peter sections:** warm and rough, with *italic* inner voice and one joke per section.
- **Narrator:** has a personality and opinions. At least **three** clear "here's where I disagree with the usual take" moments. (This matters for the policy notes in section 7.)
- **Retire these phrases**, which are generic and overused in this niche: "chess match," "Once is noise, twice is weird," "world's greatest detective" (that's also Batman's title), "this changes everything," "and that's when…". Write fresh lines.
- **Don't write in the conditional.** Use present tense: "L pulls," not "L would pull."
- **Keep L's Notes #1–#7 and the scoreboard,** but make them *specific to this episode*. Don't reuse wording from other episodes.

## 7. Originality and YouTube policy notes (for the writer and the editor)

YouTube's monetization policy on **"inauthentic content"** (renamed from "repetitious content" in July 2025) targets **mass-produced or repetitive videos**: template-based uploads with little variation, content that looks made at scale, or slideshows with little original storytelling. The separate **reused content** policy targets videos built mostly from other people's material without substantial original commentary.

What keeps this video clearly on the right side:
1. **Original story elements:** Stroud, Marco, Dre, the cable betrayal and L's turn don't exist in either franchise.
2. **Original analysis:** real-world method and real math, plus the narrator's own opinions and pushback.
3. **A human voice.** If you use AI text-to-speech, the risk is higher. A human narrator is strongly preferred, or at least a heavily directed and edited delivery.
4. **Original visuals:** commissioned or original illustrations, maps, the L's Notes cards and data graphics. Keep any film clips short, transformed and covered by narration. **No film scores.**
5. **Label dramatizations.** Anything styled as "traffic-cam" or "security footage" gets a small **"Dramatization"** tag, so nothing looks like a real event.
6. **Don't use a copy-paste series template.** Every "Could L catch ___?" episode needs its own structure, twist and original characters, not a find-and-replace of this one.
7. **Keep the Part 2 promise.** If the video says the best comment shapes Part 2, feature real commenters in Part 2.
