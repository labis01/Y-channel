# Harper Lane Films — Episode 001: Character Bible

Reference sheet for every named character in "Before I Let You Go." Use each physical
description as the **anchor prompt** for that character's first generated image — then use that
image as an image-to-image reference for every subsequent shot of the same character, same
method already proven on Logic Nexus's L portrait (pipeline note 4b). Don't re-prompt from text
alone once an anchor image exists; drift is the whole problem this method solves.

No de-risking constraints apply here (all characters are original, not franchise/trademarked),
so full likeness detail is fine everywhere — titles, prompts, thumbnail, all of it.

---

## Danielle "Dani" Carter — Lead

**Age:** mid-30s
**Physical (anchor prompt basis):** Warm brown skin, natural coily hair worn pulled back in a
low bun or head wrap while working, medium build, expressive dark eyes that read as both warm
and quietly exhausted, minimal makeup — a woman who looks like she's been holding a lot together
without much help. Photorealistic, soft naturalistic lighting (not noir/moody like Logic Nexus —
this channel's visual language is warmer, more grounded).
**Wardrobe:** Apron over a simple henley or t-shirt and jeans at the restaurant; softer, looser
clothing (cardigan, plain top) at home — the apron itself (Josephine's, inherited three
generations deep) should be a consistent recurring prop/costume piece across every restaurant
scene.
**Voice (ElevenLabs search terms):** warm adult female, grounded/conversational, needs range for
both quiet restraint and hardened resolve — not a single-note read. Avoid overly polished
"narrator" voices; this needs to sound like a person, not a host.
**Personality:** Guarded but not cold. Leads with competence. Processes grief through control —
cleaning, organizing, keeping busy — rather than visible breakdowns. Slow to anger; once angry,
precise and cutting rather than explosive.
**Speech pattern:** Short, declarative sentences under stress. Opens up into longer sentences
only with her mother. Uses dry sarcasm as a defense mechanism with Renee.
**Arc:** Cautious hope (reopening) → devastation (betrayal revealed) → grief-anger → clarity →
quiet, reclaimed strength. Ends the film more herself than she's been in three years, not
"fixed" or triumphant.

## Marcus Carter — Husband

**Age:** late 30s
**Physical:** Clean-cut, office-job presentation, tired eyes he's practiced at hiding, average
build. Clothes that look put-together at a glance but carry small tells of strain — a slightly
wrinkled collar, a missed button — consistent with money quietly draining somewhere he won't
explain.
**Wardrobe:** Loosened tie, button-down shirts. Same suit/shirt rotation should look subtly less
maintained as the story progresses (a visual, wordless tell of the unraveling).
**Voice:** Smooth, practiced-charm male voice — someone used to smoothing things over verbally.
Needs real range down into remorse/cracking by Act 2; don't cast a voice that can only do
"confident."
**Personality:** Conflict-avoidant to a fault. Genuinely loves Dani but chose the comfort of
silence over the difficulty of honesty, repeatedly, for three years. Not a cartoon villain — a
coward, specifically, which is a harder and more honest thing to play.
**Speech pattern:** Deflects with humor early in a scene. Over-explains once caught. Trails off
mid-sentence when guilt catches up to him mid-lie.
**Arc:** Comfortable deception → cornered honesty → desperate overcorrection (the Devon scene,
which makes everything worse) → quiet acceptance of consequences by Scene 13. No redemption arc
completed on-screen — he leaves the story changed but not "forgiven."

## Renee — Best Friend / Confidante

**Age:** mid-30s, same generation as Dani
**Physical:** Visual contrast to Dani — shorter natural hair or braids, more animated body
language, bolder/more colorful clothing choices. Should read as immediately distinguishable from
Dani in silhouette alone, since these two share the most screen time together.
**Voice:** Direct, energetic, grounded female voice — faster natural speaking pace than Dani's,
clearly distinct timbre so scenes between them read without needing visual cues.
**Personality:** Loyal, blunt, protective. The one who says what everyone else is thinking.
Occasional dry humor, but always pivots back to support rather than deflection.
**Speech pattern:** Quick, punchy sentences. Asks direct questions Dani is avoiding. Uses humor
to soften — never to dodge — hard truths.

---

## Isaiah — Younger Brother

**Age:** late 20s
**Physical:** Leaner, more casually dressed than Dani or Marcus — hoodies, sneakers — visually
reads as someone still figuring his life out, in contrast to Dani's grounded restaurant-owner
presence.
**Voice:** Younger adult male voice, slightly more clipped and impatient in rhythm than Marcus's.
Not aggressive — just less practiced at diplomacy.
**Personality:** Protective of Dani, expressed as bluntness rather than warmth. Own life still
unsettled, which is exactly why his instinct is "cut losses" rather than "fight for it" — he's
speaking from his own experience, not just giving bad advice.
**Speech pattern:** Interrupts. Casual, short phrasing. Deflects through self-aware humor when
confronted (Scene 7, and again in Scene 14: "I said a lot of things. I was wrong about that
one.").
**Arc:** Skeptical outside voice pushing Dani toward the easier exit → proven wrong on the
specific call to sell → redeemed by Scene 14 as genuinely present and useful, without a big
speech about it — just showing up.

## Patricia Carter — Mother

**Age:** 60s
**Physical:** Silver-streaked natural hair, dresses with quiet dignity — capable hands, the
calm bearing of someone who ran a restaurant kitchen most of her adult life.
**Voice:** Mature, warm, steady female voice. Slower natural pace than the other characters — a
grounding presence whenever she's in a scene, vocally as well as narratively.
**Personality:** The emotional anchor of the story. Not naive — she's seen hard times before
(a three-generation restaurant legacy, referenced in Scene 12: a divorce, two recessions, a
kitchen fire). Measured. Doesn't rush to hand Dani an answer.
**Speech pattern:** Longer, storytelling sentences. Grounded wisdom without tipping into cliché.
Direct exactly when it counts — Scene 12's "That's not mine to answer for you" lands because she
doesn't over-explain it.

## Devon — Single-Scene (Scene 10 only)

**Age:** late 30s/40s
**Physical:** Minimal detail needed — nondescript, low-key presence. Reads as someone from
Marcus's past he's not proud of revisiting. No need for a dedicated anchor image beyond a single
generic consistent look for the one scene.
**Voice:** Per the script's own production note — low priority to cast a dedicated ElevenLabs
voice given he doesn't reappear; a stock/library voice is fine here.
**Personality:** Not menacing — weary and transactional. Someone who has done this exact dance
with Marcus before and is tired of it, not someone looking for trouble.

## Production notes
- Generate anchor images in this priority order: **Dani first** (most screen time, appears in
  nearly every scene), then Marcus, then Renee, Patricia, and Isaiah. Devon last, lowest
  priority.
- Keep the warmer/naturalistic lighting language consistent across every character's anchor
  prompt — this channel's visual identity is deliberately distinct from Logic Nexus's
  noir/desaturated look (see CLAUDE.md format notes).
- Wardrobe continuity call-outs above (Dani's apron, Marcus's degrading shirt polish) are cheap,
  wordless storytelling — flag them to whoever's writing image prompts scene-by-scene so they
  don't get lost once production actually starts.
