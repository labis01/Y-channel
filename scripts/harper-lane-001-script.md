# Harper Lane Films — Episode 001: "Before I Let You Go"
## Full Script (scene-by-scene)

**Format note:** Screenplay-lite — scene heading, brief action line, character name, dialogue.
Action lines are kept short and non-visual where the locked constraints apply (no hospital
scenes, no blood, no depiction of loss of life — the miscarriage is referenced only through
dialogue, never shown or flashed back to). Restaurant is named **Josephine's**, after Dani's
grandmother — Patricia ran it after Josephine, and has now handed it to Dani.

Runtime target: ~40 minutes. Scene timestamps are estimates for pacing, not locked to the
second — adjust against actual ElevenLabs output once recorded, same as the Logic Nexus
pipeline's approach to pacing drift.

---

## ACT 1 — COLD OPEN / SETUP (0:00–10:00)

### SCENE 1 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — EARLY MORNING (0:00–2:00)

*Dani unlocks the front door of a small soul-food restaurant. Chairs still up on tables, a
layer of dust on the counter. She wipes a hand across the counter, looks at the dust on her
fingers, exhales.*

**DANI (to herself):**
Okay, Josephine's. Let's see if you remember how to do this.

*She flips the sign in the window from CLOSED to OPEN. Holds her hand on the glass a second
longer than she needs to.*

**DANI (to herself):**
First day back.

---

### SCENE 2 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — CONTINUOUS (2:00–4:30)

*RENEE pushes through the door, two coffees in hand, already talking before she's fully inside.*

**RENEE:**
I brought reinforcements. You didn't answer your phone, so I panicked and assumed either
you were dead or the fryer was on fire.

**DANI:**
Neither. I just didn't want to talk before I got the door open.

**RENEE:**
*(handing her a coffee)*
Fair. How's it feel?

**DANI:**
Like I'm standing in my mother's restaurant wearing my grandmother's apron, about to serve
food I haven't cooked professionally in two years.

**RENEE:**
That's not what I asked.

**DANI:**
*(quiet)*
It feels like something to do with my hands that isn't just... getting through the day.
Mama said the same thing when she took it over. Keeps you upright.

**RENEE:**
And Marcus? He coming by?

**DANI:**
Said he'd try. Work's been a lot for him lately.

**RENEE:**
*(a beat, not quite convinced)*
Been "a lot" for a few months now.

**DANI:**
Renee.

**RENEE:**
I'm just saying the timing's interesting. You're finally getting back on your feet and he's
suddenly never around to see it.

**DANI:**
He's stressed about money. That's all it is.

*Renee doesn't push further, but the look on her face says she's filing it away.*

---

### SCENE 3 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — LATER THAT DAY (4:30–6:30)

*The lunch rush has come and gone — a handful of regulars, warm chatter, the restaurant
breathing again. Dani wipes down the last table as MARCUS comes in, tie loosened, smiling but
distracted.*

**MARCUS:**
There she is. Look at this place.

**DANI:**
*(lighting up despite herself)*
You made it.

**MARCUS:**
Wouldn't miss it.
*(kisses her forehead)*
How was the first day?

**DANI:**
Good. Scary. Good.

*His phone buzzes in his pocket. He glances at it, silences it without looking, but his jaw
tightens for half a second.*

**DANI (CONT'D):**
Everything okay?

**MARCUS:**
Work stuff. Nothing.
*(too quick)*
I'm proud of you. You know that, right?

**DANI:**
You keep saying that like you're trying to convince yourself.

**MARCUS:**
*(forcing a laugh)*
I'm trying to convince you. Is it working?

**DANI:**
Ask me again once I've survived a full week.

*He pulls her into a hug. Over her shoulder, his eyes go somewhere else entirely — somewhere
worried. She doesn't see it.*

---

### SCENE 4 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — CLOSING TIME (6:30–10:00)

*Dani is alone, counting the till, humming something under her breath. She flips through the
day's mail on the counter — takeout menus, a utility bill — until she gets to one envelope.
Bank letterhead. She stops humming.*

*She opens it. Reads. Reads it again, slower, like the words might change.*

**DANI (to herself, barely audible):**
That's not — no. That can't be right.

*She sets it down, picks up her phone, and calls Marcus. It rings. And rings. Goes to
voicemail. She stares at the letter again.*

**DANI (CONT'D):**
*(to herself)*
"Notice of default." On a mortgage I didn't know we had.

*She folds the letter, slides it into her bag, and looks around the restaurant — the one
thing that was supposed to be simple, supposed to be hers — like she's seeing it differently
now.*

**DANI (CONT'D):**
*(to herself)*
First day back.

*END OF ACT 1.*

---

## ACT 2 — THE BETRAYAL UNRAVELS (10:00–25:00)

### SCENE 5 — INT. CARTER HOME — KITCHEN — NIGHT (10:00–13:00)

*Marcus comes home to find Dani sitting at the table, the letter placed dead center in front
of her, like evidence.*

**MARCUS:**
Hey — sorry, phone died at some point, I didn't even —
*(sees her face)*
What's wrong?

**DANI:**
Sit down.

**MARCUS:**
Dani, what —

**DANI:**
Sit *down*, Marcus.

*He sits. She slides the letter across the table. He looks at it. Doesn't pick it up. That's
how she knows.*

**DANI (CONT'D):**
You already know what that says.

**MARCUS:**
*(long pause)*
...Yeah.

**DANI:**
There's a second mortgage on Josephine's. My mother's restaurant. The one thing in my life
that's still standing exactly how it's supposed to. And I found out from a *default notice*.

**MARCUS:**
I was going to tell you before it got this far.

**DANI:**
When? Before or after the bank took it?

**MARCUS:**
Dani —

**DANI:**
How long.

**MARCUS:**
*(a breath)*
Three years.

**DANI:**
*(quietly devastating)*
Three *years*.

---

### SCENE 6 — INT. CARTER HOME — KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS (13:00–17:00)

**MARCUS:**
It started small. A few hands of poker with guys from work. I told myself I could stop
whenever. Then it wasn't small anymore, and I didn't know how to say it out loud without you
looking at me exactly like you're looking at me right now.

**DANI:**
So instead you took out a loan against my mother's restaurant. Without asking me. Without
telling me. Forged my —

**MARCUS:**
I didn't forge anything, I used the joint refinancing paperwork from when we bought the —

**DANI:**
Don't. Don't you dare make this about paperwork technicalities right now.

**MARCUS:**
You're right. I'm sorry.

**DANI:**
Do you know what the last two years have been for me? Do you know what it took to walk back
into that building today?

**MARCUS:**
I know.

**DANI:**
No, you don't, Marcus — because if you knew, actually *knew*, you wouldn't have spent that
whole time gambling away the one thing keeping me tethered to anything.

*Silence. Marcus has nothing that makes this better, and he knows it.*

**MARCUS:**
*(barely audible)*
I kept thinking I'd win it back before you ever had to find out.

**DANI:**
That's not a plan, Marcus. That's just — hoping the truth waits for you.

---

### SCENE 7 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — DAY (17:00–20:30)

*ISAIAH, Dani's younger brother, sits across from her at an empty table, arms crossed. She's
told him everything.*

**ISAIAH:**
So sell it.

**DANI:**
Isaiah —

**ISAIAH:**
I'm serious. Take whatever's left, get out from under the mortgage, and be done. With the
restaurant *and* with him.

**DANI:**
It's Mama's restaurant.

**ISAIAH:**
It's a building, Dani. Mama would rather you be free than broke and married to a man who lied
to you for three years.

**DANI:**
You don't know that.

**ISAIAH:**
I know Mama gave it to you to give you something to hold onto. Not something to drown in.

**DANI:**
*(sharp)*
And what exactly have you held onto lately, Isaiah? Besides opinions.

**ISAIAH:**
*(stung, but not backing down)*
That's fair. I haven't. But I'm not the one asking you to trust me with anything right now.
He is. Again.

*Dani doesn't answer. She looks at the counter, the chairs, the whole small kingdom she just
got back.*

**ISAIAH (CONT'D):**
*(softer)*
I'm not trying to pile on. I just don't want to watch you lose two things at once because you
were too busy trying to save one of them.

---

### SCENE 8 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — BACK OFFICE — LATER (20:30–23:00)

*Renee and Dani are hunched over a folder of bank statements spread across a small desk.*

**RENEE:**
Okay. This isn't three years of a few hands of poker. This is three years of a *pattern*.
Withdrawals, cash advances, a personal loan he took out in *your* name that you never signed
for —

**DANI:**
Wait, what?

**RENEE:**
*(turning the paper toward her)*
Right here. Says Danielle Carter, co-borrower. You ever sign this?

**DANI:**
*(staring at it)*
No.

**RENEE:**
Then that's not just a betrayal, Dani. That might be a legal problem for him.

**DANI:**
*(quiet, working through it)*
I don't even know what I want to do with that information yet.

**RENEE:**
You don't have to know tonight. But you should know it exists. In case you need it later.

**DANI:**
*(looking at the stack of paper)*
Three years of him lying to my face while I was —
*(stops herself)*
— while I was barely getting through the day. And he just let me think it was only the two
of us carrying something hard.

**RENEE:**
It was never just the two of you. It was you, carrying it, and him, hiding from it.

---

### SCENE 9 — INT. CARTER HOME — LIVING ROOM — NIGHT (23:00–25:00)

*Marcus and Dani, standing on opposite sides of the room. This is the fight that's been
building since Scene 5 finally boiling over.*

**MARCUS:**
I am trying to fix this, Dani. Just give me a little time.

**DANI:**
Time for what? Another loan? Another lie I find out about from a piece of mail?

**MARCUS:**
I have an idea. A real one. I can make this right before the bank even —

**DANI:**
*(cutting him off, exhausted)*
Every idea you've had for three years has made it worse. Why would this one be different?

**MARCUS:**
Because this time you know. I don't have anything left to hide.

**DANI:**
*(quiet, final for the night)*
You don't get points for finally stopping the lie once you got caught, Marcus.

*She walks out of the room. He stands alone, and for the first time, looks genuinely scared —
not of losing the restaurant. Of losing her.*

*END OF ACT 2.*

---

## ACT 3 — ROCK BOTTOM (25:00–35:00)

### SCENE 10 — INT. A QUIET CORNER OF A BAR — NIGHT (25:00–27:30)

*Marcus sits across from an old acquaintance, DEVON — someone he clearly hasn't seen in a
while, and clearly still owes something to. No cards, no chips visible — just two men and a
tense conversation.*

**DEVON:**
You said you were done with this.

**MARCUS:**
I am done. This isn't that. I need actual cash, fast, to cover a mortgage payment before the
bank moves on my wife's restaurant. I'll pay back double within the month.

**DEVON:**
Marcus. Every time you've said "one more time" it's cost you more than the last one.

**MARCUS:**
This isn't a bet, Devon. It's a loan. I'm asking you as a friend.

**DEVON:**
*(long look at him)*
Friends don't usually need this conversation twice.

**MARCUS:**
*(quiet, desperate)*
I know. I know what I'm asking. I just don't have another option left.

*Devon studies him, then slides a folded paper across the table — terms, clearly not friendly
ones.*

**DEVON:**
Fine. But this is the last time I bail you out of your own choices.

---

### SCENE 11 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — DAY (27:30–31:00)

*Dani is behind the counter when her phone rings. She answers, and her expression shifts from
neutral to alarmed over the course of the call.*

**DANI:**
*(into phone)*
Wait — say that again. Slower.
*(listening)*
The bank moved up the date? How is that even —
*(listening)*
No, I didn't authorize any new loan. What loan?

*She hangs up, stunned, and immediately dials Marcus. He picks up this time.*

**DANI (CONT'D):**
The bank just told me the account's flagged for suspicious activity. New loan inquiry under
both our names. Marcus, what did you do?

**MARCUS:**
*(over phone, guilty)*
I was trying to get ahead of it —

**DANI:**
You went and got *another* loan? After everything we just —

**MARCUS:**
It backfired. The guy — it doesn't matter who, but it's not a good situation, and now the
bank's flagged everything and they're moving up the foreclosure instead of giving us more
time.

**DANI:**
*(sitting down hard, voice flat with disbelief)*
You made it worse. You said you had a real idea, and you made it *worse*.

**MARCUS:**
Dani, I'm sorry, I panicked, I thought if I could just —

**DANI:**
Don't call me right now.

*She hangs up. Sits in the silence of the restaurant. Alone.*

---

### SCENE 12 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — NIGHT (31:00–35:00)

*Late. The restaurant is closed, lights dim except for one over the counter. Dani sits alone,
staring at nothing. PATRICIA lets herself in with her own key, sees her daughter, and quietly
sits beside her without a word for a moment.*

**PATRICIA:**
Renee called me. Told me some of it. Not all of it, I don't think, but enough.

**DANI:**
*(hollow)*
We're going to lose it, Mama. The bank moved the date up. I don't know how to stop it.

**PATRICIA:**
Baby, this restaurant survived your grandmother's divorce, two recessions, and a kitchen
fire in '99 that took out half the block. It is not going to be the thing that breaks you.

**DANI:**
I keep thinking about everything I already survived this year, and I thought this — this
place — was supposed to be the *easy* part. The part where I finally got to feel okay again.

**PATRICIA:**
It's not going to feel easy tonight. I won't lie to you about that.

**DANI:**
*(voice breaking slightly)*
I lost so much already, Mama. I don't know if I have anything left to fight with.

**PATRICIA:**
*(taking her hand)*
You don't need to fight with what you don't have. You fight with what's still standing. And
baby — look around. This is still standing. You reopened those doors two weeks ago with your
own two hands.

**DANI:**
And Marcus?

**PATRICIA:**
*(careful, honest)*
That's not mine to answer for you. But I'll say this — a man who spends three years protecting
a lie instead of protecting you isn't protecting the marriage. He's protecting himself. You get
to decide what you do with that. Not tonight. But soon.

*Dani leans her head on her mother's shoulder. No answers yet. Just the first real breath she's
taken all day.*

*END OF ACT 3.*

---

## ACT 4 — "BEFORE I LET YOU GO" (35:00–40:00)

### SCENE 13 — INT. CARTER HOME — KITCHEN — DAY (35:00–37:30)

*Marcus is packing a small bag by the door — clothes, not much. Dani stands in the doorway,
arms crossed, composed in a way that's harder-won than it looks.*

**MARCUS:**
Renee's letting me stay at her cousin's place for a bit. Figured that was easier than asking
you.

**DANI:**
Probably.

**MARCUS:**
I talked to a lawyer. About the loan under your name — I'm getting it removed. Whatever it
costs me. That part's not yours to carry.

**DANI:**
Good.

**MARCUS:**
*(a long pause)*
I'm not going to sit here and ask you to forgive three years in one conversation. I know
better than that now.

**DANI:**
That's the first genuinely honest thing you've said to me in a long time.

**MARCUS:**
I loved watching you reopen that restaurant, Dani. I meant that, even with everything else I
didn't mean.

**DANI:**
*(steady, not cruel, just done)*
I believe you loved watching it. I don't think you ever let yourself actually be *in* it with
me. Not really. Not once you started hiding things.

**MARCUS:**
Is there anything I can do?

**DANI:**
Not today. Maybe not for a long time. But you can start by not asking me to make that easier
for you.

*He nods, picks up the bag, and leaves. She stands in the empty kitchen a moment — not
triumphant, not broken. Just clearer than she's been in three years.*

---

### SCENE 14 — INT. JOSEPHINE'S — DAY, ONE WEEK LATER (37:30–40:00)

*The restaurant is full — not packed, but alive. Renee behind the counter helping out. Isaiah
wiping down a table, more useful than he's been in the whole story. Patricia seated at a
corner table like she owns the place, because in a way, she still does.*

*Dani stands at the counter, surveying it all. The default notice framed and hung behind the
counter — not as decoration, as a reminder.*

**RENEE:**
*(joining her)*
You hung it up?

**DANI:**
Every day I look at that thing, I want to remember exactly what I decided to stop tolerating.

**RENEE:**
And the bank?

**DANI:**
Refinanced. Just my name on it this time. Took every dollar I had left, plus some Mama
wouldn't let me say no to.

**ISAIAH:**
*(passing by)*
Told you. Fight for what's still standing.

**DANI:**
*(small smile)*
You said sell it, actually.

**ISAIAH:**
I said a lot of things. I was wrong about that one.

*Patricia catches Dani's eye from across the room and raises her glass slightly — no words
needed. Dani looks around Josephine's one more time: full tables, her mother's restaurant,
her name now on the paperwork, no husband standing beside her.*

**DANI (V.O.):**
I thought saving this place meant saving everything else, too. Turns out you can't hold onto
all of it. Sometimes the only way to keep the thing that matters is deciding, finally, what
you're willing to let go of first.

*She ties her apron — Josephine's apron, her grandmother's, her mother's, now hers — and walks
back into the kitchen as the lunch rush picks up around her.*

**FADE OUT.**

*END.*
